## 1. Executive Summary

When users selected **"Wear on Twin"** for custom-reconstructed 3D garments (such as the AI-reconstructed dark brown hoodie), the garment appeared floating approximately **$55\text{cm} - 90\text{cm}$ in the air above the avatar's head** rather than being draped and anatomically worn on the torso, arms, and shoulders.

This report documents the exhaustive root-cause investigation, the implementation of the production **`GarmentAvatarFittingEngine`**, the dual-mode attachment system (BVH barycentric skeletal skinning and bone-parented fallback), the clipping-plane body occlusion manager that protects the base avatar and trousers, and complete validation via automated tests and headless browser rendering.

## 2. Root Cause Audit & Anatomical Calibration

### 2.1 The Coordinate Space Mismatch
1. **Avatar World & Armature Transform**:
   - The avatar's root group (`modelRoot`) is grounded on the floor ($Y = 0\text{m}$) by setting:
     $$\text{position.y} = 0.725 \times \text{scaleY} \approx 0.904\text{m} - 0.906\text{m}$$
   - The stature scale for an avatar configured to 178cm is $\text{scale} = 1.249$.
   - Inside `modelRoot`, the humanoid armature has its origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at the **Hips**.
   - The soles of the feet in rest pose extend down to local $Y = -0.725\text{m}$ (grounding at world $Y \approx 0.015\text{m}$).

2. **Reconstructed Garment Geometry**:
   - The reconstructed 3D hoodie generated by `GarmentGeometryProcessor` was authored in **ground-relative coordinates** (measured with $Y = 0$ as the floor plane):
     - Waistband / hem: $Y = 0.88\text{m}$
     - Torso chest: $Y = 1.15\text{m}$
     - Shoulders: $Y = 1.40\text{m}$
     - Neck collar aperture: $Y = 1.46\text{m}$
     - Volumetric hood apex: $Y = 1.70\text{m}$

3. **The Fatal Attachment Flaw**:
   - In `AvatarStudioViewer.tsx`, when the garment GLB finished loading, it was attached naively:
     ```typescript
     // Naive previous code:
     const hScale = (character?.height || 1.78) / 1.70;
     garmentGroup.scale.set(hScale, hScale, hScale);
     modelRoot.add(garmentGroup);
     ```
   - Because `garmentGroup` was parented directly to `modelRoot`, Three.js computed its world-space transformation by applying `modelRoot`'s world matrix:
     $$Y_{\text{world}} = \text{modelRoot.position.y} + (\text{modelRoot.scale.y} \times Y_{\text{local}})$$
   - For the hoodie waistband at $0.88\text{m}$:
     $$Y_{\text{world}} = 0.904\text{m} + 1.249 \times (1.047 \times 0.88\text{m}) = \mathbf{2.05\text{m}}$$
   - Because the avatar's head ends at $Y = 1.55\text{m}$, the bottom hem of the hoodie was floating **$50\text{cm}$ in the air above the top of the avatar's head**, and the hood apex was hovering at $Y = 3.12\text{m}$!
   - Furthermore, the garment was parented as a static `THREE.Group` without bone weights, meaning it did not deform with the avatar's breathing cycle, turn animations, or posture adjustments.

4. **Default Clothing Occlusion Flaw**:
   - The avatar's default suit mesh (`Alex_8`, material `"Outfit"`) combines both the upper jacket and the lower trousers into a single continuous skinned mesh (28,432 vertices: 19,136 above waist, 9,296 below waist).
   - The previous code had `!isOutfit` guards that prevented hiding the suit jacket when a top was worn, or if hidden, completely stripped the avatar of its trousers.

## 4. Key Implementation Details

### 4.1 Anatomical Coordinate Alignment & Scaling
`GarmentAvatarFittingEngine` derives scale and offset from the avatar's real bind pose joints:
- **Neck Landmark**: $Y_{\text{neck}} \approx 1.280\text{m}$ (bind pose position of the `Neck` bone).
- **Waist Landmark**: $Y_{\text{waist}} \approx 0.815\text{m}$ (bind pose height of `Hips`/`Spine`).
- **Waistband Anchor**: Positioned at $Y = Y_{\text{waist}} - 0.08\text{m} \approx 0.735\text{m}$, creating a $9\text{cm}$ overlap over the trousers waistband to guarantee no bare skin is exposed.
- **Shoulder Span**: Computed as $|| \mathbf{p}_{\text{LeftArm}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{RightArm}} || \approx 0.32\text{m}$, scaled with a $1.06\times$ ease factor for hoodie fabric thickness.

### 4.2 BVH-Accelerated Collision Resolution
Garment vertices are queried against `Alex_1.geometry` in $O(\log N)$ using `three-mesh-bvh`:
```typescript
const collisionResult = GarmentCollisionResolver.resolveBodyCollisionsBVH(
  transformedPositions,
  avatar.bodyGeometry,
  { minClearanceMeters: 0.0030, stiffness: 1.0 }
);
```
Any vertex located closer than $3.0\text{mm}$ to the body surface or inside the body volume is projected outward along the true face normal.

### 4.3 Skeletal Skin Weight Transfer & Rigged Binding
Using `GarmentSkinningAdapter.transferBodySkinWeightsBVH`:
- Finds the closest body triangle for each garment vertex.
- Interpolates the 4 primary bone influences using barycentric coordinates $(u, v, w)$.
- Normalizes weights to sum strictly to $1.0$.
- Constructs a `THREE.SkinnedMesh` and binds it to `Alex_1`'s 69-bone skeleton:
  ```typescript
  const skinnedMesh = new THREE.SkinnedMesh(clonedGeometry, material);
  skinnedMesh.bind(avatar.skeleton, avatar.bodyMesh.bindMatrix);
  avatar.parentGroup.add(skinnedMesh);
  ```
- Because it shares the avatar's skeleton and bind matrix, the hoodie deforms smoothly in real-time with the avatar's idle breathing, postural sway, and interactive turn animations.

### 4.4 Smart Clipping Plane for Trousers Preservation
Because `Alex_8` is a single mesh for both the suit jacket and trousers, hiding `Alex_8` when wearing a top would strip the avatar of its trousers.
Instead of toggling boolean visibility:
- When a **Top/Hoodie** is equipped:
  A `THREE.Plane(new THREE.Vector3(0, -1, 0), 0.825)` clipping plane is attached to `Alex_8.material`. This cleanly discards all suit jacket fragments above $Y = 0.825\text{m}$ in the fragment shader while leaving the full trousers and shoes $100\%$ visible!
- The custom hoodie drapes over the waist down to $Y = 0.735\text{m}$, seamlessly overlapping the trousers waistband with zero gap.
- When unequipped, clipping planes are cleared and the original suit jacket is instantly restored.

### 4.5 Resting A-Pose Sleeve Direction Correction
In `GarmentGeometryProcessor.ts`, the procedural sleeve vector was previously pointing out at $57^\circ$ ($dX = 0.82, dY = -0.52$).
We calibrated the sleeve direction vector to match `Alex_1`'s bind-pose arm bone direction:
```typescript
// Updated in GarmentGeometryProcessor.ts:
const armDir: [number, number, number] = [side * 0.42, -0.88, 0.18];
```
This drops the sleeves naturally along the sides of the torso, terminating wrist cuffs near the avatar's hands ($Y \approx 0.86 - 0.94\text{m}$).

## 7. Known Limitations & Future Work

1. **Facial Aperture for Volumetric Cowls**:
   The procedural hood is modeled around the skull crown; future work can add dynamic morph targets for drawn-back vs pulled-over hood states.
2. **Cloth Physics Stiffness**:
   While `ClothSimulationController` is integrated for real-time fabric vibration, heavy fleece fabrics currently use high structural stiffness to prevent sagging.
3. **Multi-Layer Stacking**:
   If a user equips an inner T-shirt, an outer hoodie, and an overcoat simultaneously, `GarmentLayerManager` will apply additive thickness offsets ($3.0\text{mm}$ per layer) to prevent inter-cloth penetration.
